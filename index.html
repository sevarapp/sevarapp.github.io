About me

I grew up in Samarkand, Uzbekistan, and science became the plan at the Presidential School there, a boarding school where I studied A-Levels in math, computer science, and biology. That mix still shapes everything I do: I want to use data where it helps people the most.

Back in Uzbekistan, I helped grow the country's tech community to more than 21,000 people, coordinated a roadshow across 9 cities, and founded STEMReach Central Asia, where 95 girls built their first websites. I learned early that technology matters most when it reaches people.

Now I study data science at the University of Missouri and spend my time in research labs and at hackathons, working on healthcare, robotics, and machine learning that holds up outside the lab. Off the clock, I'm hiking, cooking, or taking photos somewhere new.

Research

Two questions I've worked on, both about catching health problems earlier.

Research assistant, Sinclair School of Nursing, May 2026 to now

Recognizing daily activities in the homes of people with dementia

Ambient sensors can show how an older adult with dementia is managing daily life at home, without cameras. I preprocess raw sensor streams and label activities, building the ground truth that transformer models learn from.

Sensor data, activity labeling, Python
Undergraduate research fellow, Scalable Data Science Lab, Fall 2025

Telling tumor from normal tissue with DNA methylation

I trained and compared machine learning classifiers, including Random Forest, to separate tumor from normal samples using DNA methylation data, then presented the poster at MU Fall Research Day.

Machine learning, genomics, poster presentation
